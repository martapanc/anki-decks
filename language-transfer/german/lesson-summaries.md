# Language Transfer — Complete German: Lesson Summaries

Topic summaries for all 50 tracks of the Language Transfer *Complete German* course, grouped into 5 thematic parts (the same grouping used for the vocabulary CSVs in this folder). This is a volunteer transcription of the course's audio segment and contains errors; track boundaries were reconstructed from content and cross-checked against page markers in the source where present.


# Part 1 · Cognates, sound shifts & first modal verbs (Tracks 1–7)

## Track 1 — Introduction & the thinking method
Overview of the "thinking method": pause, think each answer through and say it aloud, don't memorize or write anything down, work in short relaxed sessions rather than cramming. No German taught yet.

## Track 2 — English→German via -en; ich will/ich kann; P→F and vowel shifts
Many Germanic-root English verbs convert by adding **-en**: learn→**lernen**, find→**finden**, come→**kommen**. **ich will** (I want — related to "voluntary"/"amateur", not the future auxiliary "will"), **ich kann** (I can / I am able to). Consonant shift English **P→F**: hope→**hoffen**, help→**helfen**. Vowel shifts can look random but are learnable in context: sleep→**schlafen**, ripen→**reifen**, ape→**Affe**, ship→**Schiff**. **nicht** (not); **ein-** = "in-" (**einschlafen**, to fall asleep).

## Track 3 — Question inversion (kann ich...?); more hidden cognates; einschlafen
Questions are formed by inverting subject and verb, just like English: **kann ich kommen?** (can I come?). To keep the German-friendly structure, undo English contractions first — "can't I go" → "can I not go?" → **kann ich nicht gehen?**. More P→F practice (**helfen**) and more disguised cognates surface once you know the shift patterns: **reifen** (ripen), **Affe** (ape), **Schiff** (ship). Combine with **einschlafen** (to fall asleep): **ich kann nicht einschlafen**.

## Track 4 — ich komme; TH→D (danke/danken, denken); baden/Bad; Bruder; denn = because
Dropping the infinitive's **-en** gives the "I" form: kommen→**ich komme**, lernen→**ich lerne**, hoffen→**ich hoffe** (not "ich hoff"). **es** (it): **ich will es**, contracted colloquially to **ich will's**. Negation sits after the verb/object: **ich finde es nicht** (I don't find it — "I find it not"). New consonant shift English **TH→D**: thank→**danke**/**danken**, think→**denken**. **baden** (to bathe), **Bad** (bath). **Bruder** (brother, **mein Bruder** = my brother). **Ding** (thing, = English "thing"). **denn** (then/because), used like English "then" to mean "because": **kann mein Bruder kommen, denn ich kann nicht**.

## Track 5 — morgen (tomorrow/morning); no "will" needed for the future; wollen; W→V; wir
**morgen** means both "tomorrow" and "morning" (cf. Spanish *mañana*) — with a time word like this, German drops any equivalent of "will" for the future: **ich helfe morgen** = I'll help tomorrow. Confirmed sound shift: German **W** sounds like English **V** (seen in the spelling of *will*). **waschen** (to wash). **wir** (we, cf. English contraction "we're") takes the plain infinitive form: **wir kommen** = we come/we're coming. **wollen** (to want, infinitive) is irregular against **ich will** but shows the link to "voluntary" clearly. New shift: English **S** can sound like **Z** — **sehen** (to see): **wir sehen es**, **ich sehe es morgen**.

## Track 6 — Sie = you(formal)/they; word order with nicht & time words; die Kinder; singen
**Sie** (formal "you" / "they" — S sounds like Z, ie sounds like "ee") takes the plain infinitive: **Sie kommen**. Negation order: verb, then **nicht** (**Sie kommen nicht**); a time word sits before **nicht** (**Sie kommen morgen nicht**). Questions invert subject and verb for any subject, not just pronouns: **kommen Sie morgen?**, **kommen die Kinder?** — tone of voice distinguishes a statement from a question (and **gehen wir!** "let's go!" from **gehen wir?** "are we going?"). **die** (the, used here for plural/collective), **die Kinder** (the children, cf. *Kindergarten*). **singen** (to sing, S→Z again). **sie** serves for both "they" and "them" — no English-style change.

## Track 7 — mich; T→S shift (es, essen, Wasser, hassen, vergessen); müssen ≠ "must"
**mich** (me). Negation/word-order recap: **sehen Sie mich morgen nicht?** (won't you see me tomorrow?). New consonant shift English **T→S**: it→**es**, eat→**essen**, water→**Wasser** (also W→V), better→**besser**, hate→**hassen**, forget→**vergessen** (guessable once you strip **-en** and apply the shift). Idiom: **ich vergesse mich** (lit. "I forget myself") = I lose my temper/get carried away. **müssen**: **ich muss** (I must/have to); crucially **ich muss nicht** means "I don't have to", not "I mustn't" — **muss ich?** = must I?/do I have to?


# Part 2 · Modal verbs, negation & verb-final word order (Tracks 8–16)

## Track 8 — das/ist/groß; ausgehen; was/wollen; es ist
**t→s** and **d/th→d** shifts recap: **das** (this/that), **ist** (is), **groß** (big/great) → *Das ist groß*, *Das ist nicht groß*. Comparative **besser** (better); question inversion (*Ist das nicht besser?*). **aus** (out) → **ausgehen** (to go out); **denn** (because/then, "then" doubling as "because"). Playful compound: **einschlafen** (to fall asleep, "in-sleep") vs **ausschlafen** (to sleep in, "out-sleep"). **was** (what, w→v, t→s), **wollen** (to want) → *Was wollen Sie essen?*, *Was ist das?* **es** (it) → **es ist** (it is).

## Track 9 — verstehen; spät/später; kaufen/verkaufen
Recap of the shifts seen so far: P→F, T→S, TH→D, S sounding like Z, W→V, German V→F. **verstehen** (to understand, ver+**stehen** "to stand") conjugated with *ich/wir* and drilled with *nicht*, *es*, *sie/Sie*, *mich*. **spät** (late) → **Spätkauf** (convenience store, lit. "late-buy", a common Berlin sign), from which **kaufen** (to buy) is extracted. **später** (later, "late"+er, same pattern as English). **verkaufen** (to sell, ver+kaufen) and **vergessen** (to forget) show the productive **ver-** prefix. Imperative: *Verkaufen Sie es!* (Sell it!) / *Verkaufen Sie es nicht!* (Don't sell it!).

## Track 10 — senden; time expressions before nicht; übermorgen
**senden** (to send, s sounding like z). The neutral position for a time expression is right before **nicht**: *Ich kaufe es morgen nicht* (I'm not buying it tomorrow) — but it can shift for emphasis: *Ich kaufe es nicht morgen* (I'm not buying it tomorrow [I'm buying it Tuesday]). **übermorgen** (the day after tomorrow, lit. "over-tomorrow") introduces the **ü** umlaut (rounded lips held while shaping an [e] sound), alongside the earlier **ä** in *spät*.

## Track 11 — German's "value system"; verb-final word order with two verbs
Conceptual track: German's word order is framed as a system for preventing listener presumption/interruption — when a sentence has two verbs, the first verb (e.g. **will**, "want") opens the sentence, everything else is packed into the middle, and the second verb (the infinitive, e.g. **kaufen**) is saved for the very end, so the listener can't guess the full idea early. Drilled with *Ich will es kaufen* (I want to buy it) → *Ich will es morgen kaufen* (I want to buy it tomorrow).

## Track 12 — kann + nicht + infinitive-final order; bleiben; hier; ei vs ie
Extends the two-verb pattern to **nicht**: modal, then *es*, then the time expression, then **nicht**, then the infinitive last — *Ich will es morgen nicht kaufen*, *Ich kann es morgen nicht senden*. **später** reused: *Ich kann es später nicht kaufen*. New: **bleiben** (to stay, spelled with **ei** → [aɪ], as in *einschlafen*) and **hier** (here, spelled with **ie** → [iː], as in *Sie*/*die*) — contrasted so the two spellings aren't confused. Drilled: *Ich kann hier bleiben*, *Ich kann morgen nicht hier bleiben*.

## Track 13 — jetzt; können; word order with modals; verkaufen/kaufen
**jetzt** (now, related to English "yet" + an added -st). Contractions: **sie wollen's** (they want it). Modal + infinitive "sandwich": **was wollen sie jetzt essen?** (what do they want to eat now?) — the conjugated modal comes second, the infinitive closes the sentence. **können** (can/to be able, irregular **kann**; ö as in "bird"). **kaufen** (to buy) / **verkaufen** (to sell, ver- prefix). **bleiben** (to stay), **hier** (here — ie = long "ee", as opposed to ei = "i" as in bleiben). Time expressions come before **nicht**: **Ich kann jetzt nicht hier bleiben** (I can't stay here now).

## Track 14 — da; müssen; the -ieren verbs (Latin loanwords)
**da** (there; English TH → German D). **müssen** (must/have to, irregular **ich muss**, no umlaut). A wave of Latin verbs ending **-ieren** (pronounced roughly [iən]): **organisieren, studieren, adoptieren, aktivieren, argumentieren, boykottieren, autorisieren, attackieren, definieren, demolieren, deportieren, funktionieren, informieren, interessieren, kopieren, existieren**. Latin C (as in reduce, accept) → German Z (pronounced "ts"): **reduzieren, akzeptieren**. **uns** (us): **Sie müssen uns informieren** (they must inform us).

## Track 15 — informal du; dropping final -e; nichts; wollen/können recap
Colloquial dropping of the final -e on "I" forms: **ich bleibe → ich bleib**, **ich komme → ich komm** (distinct from the true irregularity of kann/will/muss). **du** (informal you, from archaic English "thou", TH→D) takes **-st**: **gehen → du gehst**, **verstehen → du verstehst**. **nichts** (nothing/anything = nicht + s), colloquially **nix**. **wollen** (to want, irregular **du willst**), **können** (irregular **du kannst**). **sehen** (to see): **Kannst du es sehen?**

## Track 16 — machen; k→ch cognates; du forms of müssen/können; bald
**machen** (to do/make) shows the English K → German CH pattern: make/**machen**, break/**brechen**, speak/**sprechen**. **du machst**, **was machst du?** (what are you doing / what do you do). The **du**-forms of irregular modals keep the singular-I irregularity minus the umlaut: **du musst**, **du kannst**. **bald** (soon): **du musst es bald machen**. Regular **-st**: **du organisierst**, **du singst** (singen, S pronounced Z).


# Part 3 · Articles, gender & noun plurals (Tracks 17–27)

## Track 17 — ein/eine; feminine -e nouns; mein/dein; ou→u; Haus, Strasse
The **du**/ou→u pattern: thou→**du**, course→**Kurs**, mouth→**Mund**, ground→**Grund**, round→**rund**. **ein** (a, masc/neuter) vs **eine** (a, feminine) — many feminine nouns end in unstressed **-e**: **eine Bluse** (a blouse), **eine Wunde** (a wound), **eine Suppe** (a soup). **mein/meine** (my), **dein/deine** (your, informal): **mein Bruder ist jung** (my brother is young), **wo ist deine Strasse?** (Strasse shows the German T→S shift, as in street/**Strasse**). **Haus** (house, not feminine → mein/dein Haus).

## Track 18 — haben; kein/keine; Kind/Kinder; the -e = feminine/plural link
**haben** (to have, **ich habe** / colloquial **ich hab'**). **kein/keine** = "no" in the sense of "not any": **das ist kein Problem**, **ich habe kein Problem**. **du hast** (irregular, historically from *habst*). The unstressed **-e** marks both feminine *and* plural — first seen with **Kind → Kinder** (child/children, an irregular plural): **meine Kinder**, **wir haben keine Kinder** (we have no children).

## Track 19 — eins/zwei; zu (to/too); ist/es; comparative -er + umlaut
**eins** (one) → **ein/eine** (a). **zwei** (two, T→Z, W→English V, EI→"i"). **zu** = "to" (as in "too quiet"), English T → German Z. **es ist warm** (it is warm), **es ist zu warm** (too warm). The comparative **-er** frequently triggers an umlaut on the stem vowel: **warm → wärmer**, **gross → grösser**, **kalt → kälter** (o→ö, a→ä; can't be added to e, i, or already-umlauted vowels).

## Track 20 — Lust; zu + infinitive ("in order to"); etwas/was
**(ich habe) Lust** (I feel like it/I'm in the mood, literally "I have desire"); with a following verb it needs **zu**: **ich habe Lust zu gehen** (I feel like going) — zu marks an implied "in order to". **etwas** (something/somewhat), often contracted to **was** in speech: **willst du was kochen?** (do you want to cook something?). More umlauted comparatives: **größer, wärmer**.

## Track 21 — suchen/versuchen/besuchen; the VER-/BE- prefixes
**suchen** (to search/seek, K→CH as in seek/suchen): **ich suche es**, **was suchst du?**. Adding **ver-** gives **versuchen** (to try) — VER- is common but its effect on meaning is unpredictable (stehen→verstehen, kaufen→verkaufen, suchen→versuchen). **ich versuche es zu machen** (I'm trying to do it — zu again for "in order to"). **besuchen** (to visit, BE- prefix): **ich besuche**, **du besuchst**.

## Track 22 — du vs. dich; warten/finden/antworten + -est
**du** (subject) vs **dich** (object) — the same I/me distinction as ich/mich: **ich verstehe dich** (I understand you). **Sie** stays the same for subject and object (formal/plural). After a stem ending in **t** or **d**, du takes **-est** instead of **-st** (for pronounceability): **warten → du wartest**, **finden → du findest**, **antworten → du antwortest**. **auf** = "on/for" with warten: **ich warte auf dich**.

## Track 23 — CH soft/hard rule; Deutsch; riechen/stinken/wissen
Recap: **ch** is soft after i/e (ich, dich, nicht) and hard after other vowels (suchen, machen); English GH/K often maps to this CH. **brechen** (to break), **sprechen** (to speak), **Deutsch** (German — spelled D-EU-T-SCH; the English word "Dutch" comes from this same word, later narrowed to the Netherlands). **riechen** (to smell, soft ch) vs **stinken** (to stink/reek). **Hund** (dog, cf. "hound"). **wissen** (to know a fact, irregular **ich weiß**, **du weißt**): **weißt du, wo mein Hund ist?**

## Track 24 — the three genders; der/die/das ↔ er/sie/es
German nouns are masculine, feminine, or neuter (English keeps this distinction only in he/she/it). **die** = "the" for feminine and plural (paralleling **sie** = she/they). **es** = "it" (neuter) ↔ **das** ("es" + D) = "the" for neuter, also "this/that": **das Kind** (neuter), **das Haus**. **der** ("the", masculine) ↔ **er** (he): **der Hund**, **der Mann**. All four pairs (er-der, sie-die, es-das) carry the same TH→D-derived consonant.

## Track 25 — noun plurals: -er, -e, -en/-n; capitalization
Three main plural patterns, reviewed together: **-er** (often with umlaut) for short neuter/masc. words — **Kind→Kinder**, **Wort→Wörter**, **Mann→Männer**; **-e** (sometimes with umlaut) — **Hund→Hunde**, **Hand→Hände**, **Wurst→Würste**, but not always — **Jahr→Jahre**; **-en/-n** for words already ending in a vowel sound — **Katze→Katzen**, **Frau→Frauen**, **Nachbar→Nachbarn**. The **-e** ending is ambiguous between feminine singular and plural (die Katze/die Katzen look alike without context/umlaut). All German nouns are capitalized, partly to keep plural nouns in -en distinct from verbs.

## Track 26 — Nachbar; -heit = -hood; more d/t and gh/ch cognates
**Nachbar** (neighbour, GH→CH as in neighbor/Nachbar; literally "near-builder": nach = towards/near, bauen = to build), plural **Nachbarn**. **lachen** (to laugh), **laut** (loud): **mein Nachbar ist zu laut**. **-heit** = English **-hood**, always feminine, another D→T shift: **Kindheit** (childhood). More d↔t / gh↔ch pairs: **Garten**/garden, **Wort**/word, **Tochter**/daughter, **Schulter**/shoulder (+ou→u), **trinken**/drink, **tanzen**/dance (Latin C→Z again). **bitten** (to ask/ask for; "please" = **bitte**; cf. English "I bid you"), **fahren** (to travel/ride, as in "farewell" = "travel well"): **meine Nachbarn fahren nach Berlin**. **Ich bitte dich zu kommen** (I'm asking you to come).

## Track 27 — he/she/it verb forms (-t); tun; es tut mir leid
Just as English adds -s for he/she/it, German adds **-t** to the bare stem: **kommen → er kommt**, **bleiben → sie bleibt**, **besuchen → er besucht**, **verstehen → er versteht**, **machen → es macht**, **stinken → es stinkt**, **riechen → es riecht**. **es macht nichts** = "it doesn't matter" (literally "it makes nothing"). **tun** (to do, from English "do", D→T): **es tut mir leid** (I'm sorry, literally "it does to me suffering") — **mir**/**dir** = "to me"/"to you" (parallel to mich/dich). **meine Schulter tut weh** (my shoulder hurts).


# Part 4 · Separable verbs & the perfect (past) tense (Tracks 28–40)

## Track 28 — scheinen/erscheinen; klären/erklären; müssen
**scheinen** (to shine, also "to seem": es scheint so = it seems so). The prefix **ER-** shifts meaning fairly randomly: **erscheinen** (to appear, to come out — a film/book), **klären** (to clarify/sort out) → **erklären** (to explain). **der Film** (masc.), **bald** (soon; final -d sounds like -t). **wir müssen** (we must). Object-pronoun word order: **kannst du es mir erklären?** (can you explain it to me — lit. "...it to-me explain").

## Track 29 — sagen; y→g shift; was/etwas; nicht→nichts; uns
**sagen** (to say) shows the **Y→G** English/German consonant shift (say/saga ~ sagen). **er sagt** (he says), **sie sagt mir** (she's telling me), **sie sagt es mir** (she's telling it to me). **was** (what, w→v and t→s shifts), **etwas** (something) — not a "little word," so it goes after the verb: **sie sagt mir etwas**. **-st** ending for "you": **was sagst du mir?**. **nicht** (not) + **-s** → **nichts** (nothing/anything): **du musst mir nichts sagen**. **uns** (us/to us): **sie sagen uns nichts**.

## Track 30 — man sagt; wie; machen-family separable verbs; darf vs muss
**man sagt** (one says — used for "how do you say..." to avoid sounding personal), **wie** (how), **auf Deutsch** (in German): **wie sagt man es auf Deutsch?** (question inversion). **machen** (to do/make) spins off separable prefix-verbs, a very Germanic feature also seen in English phrasal verbs (make up/make out/make for): **aufmachen** (open, "es ist auf" = it's open), **zumachen** (close), **anmachen** (turn on). **ich darf** (I may/I'm allowed) vs **ich muss** (I must): negated, **darf nicht** = mustn't/not allowed, but **muss nicht** = don't have to — a key distinction. **noch nicht** (not yet).

## Track 31 — sehen-family; legen; -heit = -hood (feminine)
**wie geht's?** (how are you, contraction of geht + es). **sehen** (to see) branches into **ansehen** (look at), **zusehen** (watch), **aussehen** (to look — ich will gut aussehen), **auf Wiedersehen** (goodbye, lit. "until seeing again"), **übersehen** (overlook), **vorsehen**/**Vorsicht** (caution — ch↔gh shift). **abmachen** (remove; also "to agree on"). **legen** (to lay/put, another Y→G shift: lay→legen) → **die Gelegenheit** (opportunity, from legen + **-heit** = "-hood," and -heit words are feminine: **die Kindheit** = childhood). **auflegen** (hang up the phone), **auslegen** (display; also to front someone money to be repaid).

## Track 32 — Modal verbs present tense; nehmen; mitnehmen/mitkommen
Full modal conjugation, all sharing an ich/du irregularity that then carries to er/sie/es: **wollen** (want: ich will, du willst, wir wollen), **können** (can: ich kann, du kannst, wir können — ö like the vowel in "bird"), **müssen** (must: ich muss, du musst — loses the umlaut), **wissen** (to know a fact: ich weiss/du weisst — weiss also = "white"). **nehmen** (to take) + **mit** (with) → **mitnehmen** (to take away). **dürfen** (to be allowed: ich darf, du darfst) + **mitkommen** (to come with/accompany): **darf er mitkommen?**

## Track 33 — The perfect tense with haben; ge-...-t
Spoken German overwhelmingly prefers the **haben + participle** perfect even where English would use simple past ("did you laugh?" = "have you laughed?"). Build the participle from the **er/sie/es** present-tense form + **ge-** prefix: **lachen→lacht→gelacht** (ich habe gelacht), **machen→macht→gemacht** (ich habe es gemacht — object goes between habe and gemacht). **du hast**, **er hat** (irregular short forms of haben). **warum** (why): **warum hat er es noch nicht gemacht?**

## Track 34 — gestern; irregular participles resembling English (bringen, verstehen)
**gestern** (yesterday, y→g shift). Practice: **kaufen→gekauft** (ich habe es gekauft). Some irregular participles are "ancient" shared irregularities with English once you apply the sound shifts: **bringen→gebracht** (brought — gh↔ch shift makes "bracht"/"brought" nearly identical), **verstehen→verstanden** (understood): **ich habe verstanden**. **nichts** doubles for "anything" in a negative context: **ich habe nichts verstanden** (I haven't understood anything).

## Track 35 — More perfect-tense practice; wie viel; gefunden/gesehen; ihn
**warten→gewartet** (an extra -e- is inserted before -t since the stem already ends in -t: er wartet, er hat auf mich gewartet — "auf mich" = for me). **kosten→gekostet**: **wie viel kostet es?** (how much does it cost — verb-first question inversion), **wie viel hat es gekostet?** (how much did it cost). More English-like irregular participles: **finden→gefunden** (found), **sehen→gesehen** (seen: ich habe gesehen). **ihn** (him): **ich habe ihn gestern gesehen** (I saw him yesterday).

## Track 36 — sie/er/ihn; für; separable vs. inseparable prefixes (accent rule)
**sie** covers she/her *and* they/them unchanged; **er** (he)/**ihn** (him). **für** (for) — the umlaut Ü is a U-shape mouth pronouncing E: **ich habe es für ihn gemacht**. The **suchen**-family (**versuchen** = to try, **besuchen** = to visit, **aussuchen** = to pick out) illustrates the rule for whether a prefix separates: if the prefix is a standalone word and carries the stress (**auf-**, **an-**, **aus-**), it splits off to the end of the clause in a finite sentence (**ich mache es morgen auf** = I'm opening it tomorrow); if the prefix isn't a word on its own and is unstressed (**ver-**, **be-**), it never separates (**ich versuche**, **ich besuche ihn morgen**). **ankommen** (to arrive) splits the same way: **ich komme morgen an**, **wir wollen morgen ankommen** (stays whole after a modal, since ankommen is still in its "to" form).

## Track 37 — Separable vs. non-separable verbs in the past; anmachen/ausmachen, versuchen/besuchen
Confirms the split-verb past rule: a movable prefix (an-, aus-) makes way for **ge-** (**angemacht**, turned on; **ausgesucht**, chosen), while an unmovable prefix (ver-, be-) blocks **ge-** and the verb just takes **-t** (**versucht**, tried; **besucht**, visited; **verkauft**, sold). **machen** (to do/make), **suchen** (to search), **kaufen/verkaufen** (to buy/sell), **warum** (why), **heute** (today).

## Track 38 — The "have past" rule; irregular participles; schon/noch nicht/nichts; genders of "it"
The regular "have past" is built from the he/she/it form (drop -en, keep -t) plus **ge-**: **machen → gemacht**, **warten → gewartet** (wait **auf** someone, not "for"). Irregular participles often mirror English irregulars: **bringen→gebracht** (brought), **sehen→gesehen** (seen), **essen→gegessen** (eaten). **schon** = already/yet, **noch nicht** = not yet, **noch nichts** = nothing yet. "It" depends on gender: **es** (das Telefon), **ihn** (der Fernseher), **sie** (die Lampe).

## Track 39 — Non-separable prefixes and -ieren Latin verbs skip ge-; gestern/vorgestern
Non-separable verbs (**suchen→besucht/versucht**) and Latin loanverbs ending **-ieren** (**studieren, organisieren, korrigieren, servieren, kollaborieren, intervenieren**) both form the past with just **-t**, no **ge-** — German seems to treat -ieren verbs like it treats non-splittable prefixed verbs. **lange** (long), **gestern** (yesterday), **vorgestern** (the day before yesterday, "over-yesterday" pattern), **alles** (everything/all).

## Track 40 — kapieren, reparieren; der Zug vs. die Bahn; nehmen; morgen/übermorgen
More -ieren verbs: **kapieren** (to get/understand, from Latin "capture"), **reparieren** (to repair). Same word for train has two genders depending on the word chosen: **der Zug** (from ziehen, to pull) vs. **die Bahn** (track, also used for train) — a reminder that grammatical gender belongs to the *word*, not the *object*, so "it" (ihn/sie) depends on which word you had in mind. **nehmen** (to take), **morgen** (tomorrow), **übermorgen** (the day after tomorrow, "over-tomorrow").


# Part 5 · The dative case, pronouns & indirect objects (Tracks 41–50)

## Track 41 — Dative pronouns: mir/dir/ihm/ihr ("to me/you/him/her") vs. accusative mich/dich/ihn
Introduces the dative personal pronouns: **mich**→**mir** (to me), **dich**→**dir** (to you), **ihn**→**ihm** (to him), **sie**→**ihr** (to her). **sagen** (to say/tell) takes a dative recipient: **Was hast du ihr gesagt?** (What did you tell her?). **vermachen** (to leave behind/bequeath), **das Haus** (house), **etwas** (something), **aber** (but).

## Track 42 — geben; v↔b shift; dative recipients; weggeben; numbers
**geben** (to give, **gegeben** past) shows the **v↔b** consonant shift also seen in **leben** (live), **lieben** (love), **glauben** (believe), **Rabe** (raven). Recipients of "give" take dative (**mir, dir, ihm, ihr, uns, ihnen**). **weggeben** (to give away, separable — "weg" is stressed, from the same root as English "way"), past **weggegeben**. **sieben** (seven), **Kind** (child, final -d hardens to a t sound).

## Track 43 — helfen and vergeben take the dative; fragen is a surprise exception (accusative)
**helfen** (to help) and **vergeben** (to forgive, non-separable **ver-**, past = **vergeben** unchanged) both take a dative object (**mir**, not **mich**) — German frames these as "help/forgive *to* someone." **fragen** (to ask, from the same root as English "pray"; p↔f shift) breaks the pattern and takes the plain accusative (**mich, dich, ihn**), not dative — a reminder that each verb's own logic must be checked rather than assumed. **niemand** (nobody).

## Track 44 — uns; sprechen and its compounds; versuchen...zu; schon vs. schön
**wir→uns** (us, unchanged for "to us" too). **sprechen** (to speak, k↔ch shift like machen/suchen). **ansprechen** (separable: to bring up in conversation, or "to appeal to/speak to" someone — here the "to" meaning already lives in **an-**, so the person takes accusative **mich**, not dative). **aussprechen** (to pronounce). Past **gesprochen**. Contrast **schon** (already) with **schön** (beautiful) — same sounds, different words. **gut** (good/well), **versprechen** (to promise).

## Track 45 — brechen; der Lehrer; unterbrechen; the accusative article emerges (der→den, dem)
**brechen** (to break; colloquially **kaputtmachen**), past **gebrochen** (mirrors English "broken"). **lehren** (to teach), **der Lehrer** (teacher). **unterbrechen** (to interrupt) doesn't split — stress stays on -brechen, not unter-. Introduces case marking on the definite article for masculine nouns: **der Lehrer** (he/subject) → **den Lehrer** (him/direct object, from *d + ihn*) → **dem Lehrer** (to him, from *d + ihm*). **immer** (always).

## Track 46 — Stem vowel changes (e→i); die Lehrerin; feminine dative der; es gibt
Recaps the e→i stem shift for du/er with **brechen→bricht**, **sprechen→spricht**, **geben→gibt** (but no change for the plural: **sie geben**). **die Lehrerin** (female teacher, **-in** suffix). Feminine nouns don't distinguish subject/object (**sie** = she/her, **die** stays **die**) but do change for dative: **die Lehrerin → der Lehrerin** (to her, from *d + ihr*). **es gibt** = "there is/are" (literally "it gives"); question form **Gibt es...?**; **noch** = still/any left.

## Track 47 — der Koch/die Köchin; ein→einen; der Aufzug; full der/den/dem review
**der Koch / die Köchin** (cook, with Umlaut in the feminine). **ankommen** (to arrive, separable). The indefinite article takes the same accusative shift as *der*: **ein Zug → einen Zug** (from *ein + ihn*), e.g. **Es gibt einen Zug** (there is a train). **der Aufzug** (elevator, "up-pull"). Full recap: masculine **der/den/dem**, feminine **die/die/der** — case depends on how the noun "behaves" (subject / direct object / recipient), for any noun, not just people.

## Track 48 — Freund/Freundin; possessives inflect like ein; plural Freunde; dative plural ihnen
**ein Freund / eine Freundin** (friend, also boyfriend/girlfriend). Possessives (**mein, dein**) case-inflect exactly like *ein*: **meinen Freund** (accusative), **meiner Freundin** (dative feminine), **deinem Freund** (dative masculine). Plural **die Freunde** (-e plural). **ihnen** = to them (built like *ihn* + plural -en). **senden** (to send), **die E-Mail**.

## Track 49 — schreiben; die Kinder/Köche; the dative plural takes -n on the noun too; müssen
**schreiben** (to write). Plural nouns **die Kinder** (children, -er plural) and **die Köche** (cooks, -e plural + Umlaut). In the dative plural, both the article and the noun pick up an -n (from **ihnen**): **die Kinder → den Kindern**, **die Köche → den Köchen**. **du wartest** (an extra -e- before -st when the stem ends in -t). **müssen** (must/have to, Umlaut).

## Track 50 — sein (to be); dick/dünn; weekdays; motion verbs use sein, not haben, in the past
**sein** (to be): **ich bin, du bist, er/sie/es ist, wir/sie sind** — as irregular as English "am/is/are." **dick** (fat) / **dünn** (thin), th↔d shift; **so** (so), **zu** (too). Weekdays built from recognisable roots: **Donnerstag** (Thursday, "thunder's day"), **Montag** (Monday, moon day), **Mittwoch** (Wednesday, midweek), **Sonntag** (Sunday, sun day), used with **am** (on). Verbs of motion/change-of-location (**gehen, kommen, ausgehen**) form the perfect with **sein** rather than **haben**: **ich bin gegangen** (I have gone/I am gone), **bist du ausgegangen?** (did you go out?).
